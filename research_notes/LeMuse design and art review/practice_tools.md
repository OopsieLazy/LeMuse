# Timed drawing-practice and reference-viewing tools, and the teaching behind them

Research date: 2026-11-19. Covers sources found during this pass. Many tool pages are old or undated; ages are marked. No personal data is included. Forum user handles are left out on purpose.

## 1. Which tools lead, and what they offer

### Takeaway
The timed-practice field is split. **Session timers** (Line of Action, QuickPoses, SketchDaily, Posemaniacs, Proko Timer, Art in Context Pose Reference, many small mobile apps) show one image at a time and run a class-mode progression. **Reference boards** (PureRef) are free-form canvases with greyscale, grids and colour picking but no timer. The most complete free web timer documented in this pass is Art in Context's Pose Reference. It bundles class mode, custom steps, mirror, greyscale, 3×3/4×4 grid, back, pause, skip, audio countdown beeps, an end-of-session summary and an opt-in nude filter, which is close to LeMuse's planned feature set.

### Cited Findings

**Line of Action (web; ads unless subscribed)**
- Class mode "will warm you up with quick gesture drawings, gradually ease you into longer poses, and even have built in break times during long classes". Skip and pause exist, and the background turns black "to reduce distractions". The source article is dated 25 May 2011, so it is old — [Line of Action: Completely revamped](https://line-of-action.com/article/completely-revamped-new-features)
- The site's own interview says class mode starts at 30-second drawings and increases pose length, reaching up to 2 hours at the longest setting. Third-party guides say classes run from 30 minutes to 6 hours — [search summary: LoA interview / third-party guides](https://line-of-action.com/article/an-interview-about-the-site); [wherecreativityworks](https://wherecreativityworks.com/line-of-action/)
- A user describes a 30-minute class as 10 × 30 s, 5 × 1 min, 2 × 5 min, then 1 × 10 min. This is a user's account, not an official table — [LoA forum: 30 min Gesture Class](https://line-of-action.com/forums/topic/30-min-gesture-class)
- A site update made class-mode timing "more exact" and shows how many poses are left in each duration. The update is undated — [search summary of LoA pages](https://line-of-action.com/forums/topic/30-min-gesture-class)
- Users compare the modes. Standard mode lets you plan your own timing and class mode sets the schedule automatically, "like a live drawing session". One user does mostly short Standard poses plus a ~30-minute Class weekly. A beginner who felt "rushed" in Class mode uses Standard at ~10 min per image — [LoA forum: Standard vs Class mode](https://www.line-of-action.com/forums/topic/benefits-of-standard-mode-vs-class-mode)
- Feature requests, Jan–Mar 2024:
  - a **larger timer in class mode** and an **optional warning sound when little time remains**;
  - male/female-only filters and more nonbinary models;
  - **saved preferences** for session type, length and models;
  - a filter by photographer;
  - new categories (plants, machinery/vehicles);
  - themed challenges.

  The developer said "major upgrades" were planned for 2024 — [LoA forum: next version](https://line-of-action.com/forums/topic/what-would-you-like-to-see-in-the-next-version-of-line-of-action)
- A user complaint: the on-screen timer is too small, which matters most for poses of a minute or less — [search summary, LoA forums](https://line-of-action.com/forums/topic/what-would-you-like-to-see-in-the-next-version-of-line-of-action)
- Reviewers' comparison: LoA shows images for 30 s–10 min, has "no untimed option", and sits next to an artist forum for feedback. It is described as behaving "very much the same as QuickPoses" — [pcweenies](https://pcweenies.com/?p=9667); [conceptartempire](https://conceptartempire.com/gesture-pose-photo-websites/)
- The About page says a set of standing poses is "fully covered" and suitable for anyone "including classroom usage" — [LoA About](https://line-of-action.com/about)
- An open-source desktop clone of Line of Action exists on GitHub — [QuevynRohel/Line-of-action](https://github.com/QuevynRohel/Line-of-action/)

**QuickPoses (web + desktop app)**
- Modes:
  - "Challenges": 20 images per session, timed;
  - "Timed practice": pick a pose type and time limit; the image changes when time runs out;
  - "Random poses": untimed.

  Free, with optional tips of €3, €5 or €10. **A desktop app costs €15.** The site is in English and Russian — [quickposes.com](https://quickposes.com/)
- Often named as the top pick. One reviewer calls the site "less well-made visually" but says it has "more options of what to draw" — [conceptartempire](https://conceptartempire.com/gesture-pose-photo-websites/)
- The QuickPoses desktop app can sample **local image files** (including bought Proko packs), which tablets and phones can't do — [Proko forum: Timer Tool improvements](https://proko.com/community/topics/timer-tool-improvements)

**Quick Poses (iOS app, separate developer)**
- Simulates "academic figure drawing sessions". Pose duration runs from 5 s to infinity, and you can cap the session length by limiting the number of poses. A summary shows progress at the end. The developer **replaced the "end session" button with a "pause" button** to make clear it doesn't end the session — [App Store: Quick Poses](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1510309896)

**Proko Timer (web, with Proko's paid reference packs)**
- Picks photos at random from the packs you own and shows them full screen for a set time. It had a 25-image-per-session limit (~3 years ago). Keyboard controls were added later:
  - ← / → to go back or forward;
  - Space to play or pause;
  - Enter to restart the current image's timer;
  - "?" to show the controls.

  — [Proko forum: Timer Tool improvements](https://proko.com/community/topics/timer-tool-improvements)
- Users asked for:
  - **class mode** (the timer increases after a few images, "like Quickposes");
  - more than 25 images;
  - **the total session length shown before starting** ("30s images, 40 images total = 20 mins session");
  - **greyscale and upside-down** display.

  Staff said the requests were forwarded. No confirmation of implementation was found — [Proko forum: Timer Tool improvements](https://proko.com/community/topics/timer-tool-improvements); [Proko: Suggestion – Timed figure drawing tool](https://proko.com/community/topics/suggestion-timed-figure-drawing-tool)
- Packs are labelled for content. Some are uncensored ("contains nudity") and the anatomy pack is censored. A user asked for a nude/clothed filter, and none was confirmed — [search summary, Proko community](https://www.proko.com/community/topics/online-tools-for-practice)

**Art in Context — Pose Reference (free web)**
- Timer:
  - Same time for every pose: 30 s, 1, 2, 5 or 10 min, or no limit; sessions of 5–50 poses.
  - Class mode presets:
    - **Gesture warm-up, 10 min**: 10 × 30 s + 5 × 1 min;
    - **20 min**: + 2 × 5 min;
    - **30 min**: + 1 × 10 min;
    - **1 h**: 10 × 30 s, 5 × 1 min, 5 × 2 min, 2 × 5 min, 3 × 10 min.
  - Custom: up to 8 steps, each with 1–99 poses at 15 s–30 min.
  - Untimed "one random pose".
- Timing behaviour:
  - **The countdown starts only after the image loads.**
  - **The session pauses when you switch tabs.**
- Filters:
  - pose type: standing, sitting, kneeling/lying, action, hands, faces;
  - model: anyone, women, men;
  - source: **"Photos only" or "Drawings & art only"**;
  - **nudes hidden unless "Include nude figure studies" is ticked, and that choice is deliberately not saved.**
- In-session tools:
  - Mirror (F) and Greyscale (B);
  - Grid (G): 3×3 or 4×4;
  - Back (restarts that pose's timer), Pause (Space), Skip (→), full screen, End.
- Sound: three short beeps in the last 3 s, and a higher tone when the pose changes.
- End of session: a summary of the pose count, total drawing time and **every pose with its source**.

— [Art in Context: Pose Reference](https://artincontext.org/pose-reference/)

**SketchDaily reference tool (free web)**
- Serves photos by a few filters, with no search for specific poses. Options include nudity, gender, a class mode, and stationary vs action poses. Views include clothed, nude, "muscle" and "smooth". Sections cover figures, animals, and hands & feet — [conceptartempire](https://conceptartempire.com/gesture-pose-photo-websites/); [search summary, Proko community](https://www.proko.com/community/topics/online-tools-for-practice); [doncorgi](https://doncorgi.com/blog/free-pose-reference-websites/)

**Posemaniacs "Thirty Seconds" (free web, 3D models)**
- Interval: unlimited, or 10/15/30/45/60/90/180/300 s. Repeat count: 10–100 poses.
- Display options: **negative space**, **grid** (off, 4, 9 or 16 cells), bounding box (beta), floor and floor grid, shadow.
- Models: unisex, male or female. Difficulty: easy or normal.
- **History keeps up to 100 poses for review.**
- The page's tips: start with the overall structure, match detail to the time, **review your drawings afterwards**, and shorten or lengthen the time depending on whether you can capture the whole body.

— [Posemaniacs: About Thirty Seconds Drawing](https://www.posemaniacs.com/thirtysecond)

**Croquis Cafe (paid membership)**
- Offers "Life Drawing Videos … timed poses with live art models", a gallery of 25,000 fine-art photos, and an annual February challenge called "Figuary" (one video session a day) — [croquis.cafe](https://croquis.cafe/)
- Its videos are short poses (under 5 min) — [gumroad listing](https://demetriocran.gumroad.com/l/fig21demos). This is a weak source.

**PureRef (desktop reference board; Windows, macOS, Linux)**
- 2.0 (May 2024) has a canvas-wide **Grayscale** (Canvas › Grayscale, Ctrl+Alt+G) and colour picking from images. It runs on Windows 10+, Ubuntu 20.04 and macOS 10.14+. Personal licences are pay-what-you-want — [PureRef 2.0 handbook](https://pureref.com/handbook/2.0/features/); [CG Channel 2.0](https://www.cgchannel.com/2024/05/pureref-2-0-is-out)
- 2.1 (Feb 2026) adds:
  - geometric shapes (circles, rectangles, lines) alongside freehand drawing;
  - a **background grid** (lines or dots) with snapping;
  - a recent-files view;
  - batch non-destructive image optimisation;
  - six new UI languages.

  — [CGPress: PureRef 2.1](https://cgpress.org/archives/pureref-2-1-adds-shapes-grids-and-language-support.html)
- One report says the website's "custom amount" now defaults to $20 and the minimum appears to be about $7. This is secondary and unconfirmed — [search summary of CG Channel 2.1 coverage](https://www.cgchannel.com/2026/02/pureref-2-1-is-out/)

**Small and indie apps (evidence of baseline expectations)**

### Inferences
- The **baseline in 2025–2026** for a timed reference tool is:
  - class mode with a 30 s warm-up that grows to longer poses, plus custom steps;
  - pause, skip and back;
  - flip and greyscale;
  - a grid;
  - keyboard shortcuts;
  - an optional audio countdown.

  LeMuse's planned set matches it. **Missing from LeMuse's list compared with the leaders:**
  - back/previous;
  - "restart this pose's timer";
  - a total-session-length readout;
  - audio cues;
  - an end-of-session contact sheet;
  - an untimed mode;
  - negative-space and vertical-flip views;
  - saved session presets.
- **The timer starts on image load (Art in Context).** This matters for LeMuse, because images stream from museum and Are.na CDNs. A pose whose image takes 3 s to load would otherwise lose 10% of a 30 s pose. LeMuse should also pause the timer when the window loses focus or is minimised.
- **Source filter "Drawings & art only" vs "Photos".** Museum open-access images are mostly artworks (drawings, paintings, sculpture), not photographed models. Art in Context already treats "drawings & art" as a valid source for timed figure study, which supports LeMuse's master-study angle. But users expecting LoA-style model photos will find museum pools uneven in pose clarity.
- LeMuse's example "10 × 30 s then 5 × 2 min" differs from the most common preset, **10 × 30 s + 5 × 1 min** (both LoA user reports and Art in Context). Shipping the familiar presets as defaults would match expectations.
- Proko's 25-image cap and its users' requests show that pool size and local-file sampling matter. LeMuse's Are.na channels are effectively user-curated pools, which is a differentiator that QuickPoses Desktop and PureRef serve only with local folders.

### Gaps
- **Not researched in depth for lack of time or sources:** SenshiStock's tools, AdorkaStock, Figurosity, "Reference Angle", and a "Characters" app class mode. Only name mentions turned up, with no feature lists or pricing.
- **Line of Action's** current (2025–2026) feature set (flip/greyscale, end-of-session review, nudity settings, subscription price) couldn't be confirmed from primary pages.
- **Drawabox's** site returned 403 to automated fetching, so its exercise guidance and "50% rule" aren't cited here.
- **No Reddit threads** (r/learnart, r/ArtFundamentals) or Play Store reviews were retrievable. The praise and complaints above come from tool forums, app listings and blogs.
- **PureRef:** flip/mirror and its commercial licence price weren't confirmed.

## 2. Session structures teachers recommend

### Takeaway
The consensus is a warm-up of many 30 s–1 min gestures, then fewer 2, 5 and 10+ minute poses, with breaks in long sessions. This mirrors the live life-drawing class. Instructors stress that short poses are about capturing the whole action and line confidence, not finish, and that beginners may need longer times at first.

### Cited Findings
- Line of Action describes class mode as "warm you up with quick gesture drawings, gradually ease you into longer poses … built in break times during long classes". Users say it "resembles a live drawing session" and forces faster, more decisive strokes for line confidence — [LoA article, 2011](https://line-of-action.com/article/completely-revamped-new-features); [LoA forum](https://www.line-of-action.com/forums/topic/benefits-of-standard-mode-vs-class-mode)
- Typical class progressions:
  - 10 × 30 s, 5 × 1 min, 2 × 5 min, 1 × 10 min (a 30-min class) — [LoA forum](https://line-of-action.com/forums/topic/30-min-gesture-class);
  - 1 h: 10 × 30 s, 5 × 1 min, 5 × 2 min, 2 × 5 min, 3 × 10 min — [Art in Context](https://artincontext.org/pose-reference/)
- Proko (Stan Prokopenko) gesture lessons:
  - a few lines can capture a 30 s pose; try 45 s–1 min if 30 s feels too fast;
  - spend "10–15 seconds to just look and analyze the pose" before drawing;
  - over-analysing contours makes you run out of time;
  - for 2-minute poses "the point of the exercise is not to draw as fast as you can".

  — [Proko: How to Draw Gesture – Step by Step](https://proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-gesture-step-by-step); [Proko: Quicksketch 2-minute poses](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/quicksketch-assignment-examples-2-minute-poses)
- Proko community advice adds 30 s and 2 min gestures to a daily warm-up. One learner started at 2 minutes because "the timer was making me feel pressured" — [Proko: Gesture Practice](https://proko.com/community/topics/gesture-practice-1)
- Fast gesture poses help an artist "loosen up" and avoid a stiff drawing style — [Wikipedia: Gesture drawing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gesture_drawing)
- For 30 s–1 min poses, the advice is to get the pose right with a good line of action, using long flowing lines — [LoA forum: improving gesture](https://www.line-of-action.com/forums/topic/how-can-i-improve-my-gesture-drawings)
- Posemaniacs: adapt the duration to skill. If you can draw the whole body well, shorten the time; if not, lengthen it — [Posemaniacs](https://www.posemaniacs.com/thirtysecond)
- **Notan:** Proko teaches notan as a two-value study ("anything lighter goes into lights, anything darker into darks") and runs a "Notan master thumbnails" project, which combines value study with master copies — [Proko: Project – Notan Master Thumbnails](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/project-notan-master-thumbnails/downloads)

### Inferences
- LeMuse should ship **named presets** (Warm-up 10 min, 30-min class, 1-h class) plus a custom step builder. It should also have a **slow/beginner preset** (e.g. 2 min → 5 min) for people who find 30 s stressful.
- Value and notan studies are usually longer, untimed or 5–15 min exercises paired with master works. Museum open access (Cleveland) suits a "master study" preset: few images, long durations, value view on.

### Gaps
- **No primary sources retrieved** for Glenn Vilppu, Andrew Loomis, George Bridgman, New Masters Academy or sight-size practice (Atelier methods). Their timing and progression advice is not cited here.
- Drawabox (warm-ups, "50% rule" on dedicating half of drawing time to drawing for fun) is not cited because of a 403.

## 3. Image adjustments artists use, and why

### Takeaway
Flip/mirror, greyscale, squint/blur, reducing to a few values (notan, posterize), grids and negative-space views all serve one purpose: breaking perceptual habituation and checking big shapes, values and proportions. Published psychology links drawing errors to misperception.

### Cited Findings
- Proko on "tunnel vision": focusing on one area distorts value judgement. **Flip the drawing, use a mirror, tilt your head or the drawing, or lean back and squint** to reset perception. "Squinting helps you see overall value relationships and maintain correct shadow values" — [Proko: How to See Values Correctly](https://proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-see-values-correctly/notes)
- Notan reduces an image to light and dark at a chosen threshold to judge big shapes — [Proko: Notan Master Thumbnails](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/project-notan-master-thumbnails/downloads)
- Psychology research lists misperception of the object among the sources of drawing errors. Accurate drawing relates to accurate bottom-up perceptual processing, which flip and squint tricks target — [Chamberlain & Wagemans, Errors in drawing](https://eprints-gro.gold.ac.uk/20010/1/Chamberlain%20%26%20Wagemans%20-%20Errors%20in%20drawing%20pre%20formatting%20.pdf); [Ostrofsky et al. 2012](https://stockton.edu/social-behavioral-sciences/documents/ostrofsky2012.pdf)
- Tools that implement these adjustments:
  - mirror, greyscale and 3×3/4×4 grids — [Art in Context](https://artincontext.org/pose-reference/);
  - **negative space**, grids of 4/9/16 cells and a bounding box — [Posemaniacs](https://www.posemaniacs.com/thirtysecond);
  - canvas greyscale and colour picking — [PureRef 2.0 handbook](https://pureref.com/handbook/2.0/features/);
  - a background grid — [PureRef 2.1](https://cgpress.org/archives/pureref-2-1-adds-shapes-grids-and-language-support.html);
  - vertical flip — [PoseFlow devlog](https://onirou.itch.io/poseflow/devlog);
  - upside-down display, requested — [Proko forum](https://proko.com/community/topics/timer-tool-improvements)

### Inferences
- LeMuse's planned mirror, greyscale, value/notan and grid tools match practice. **Consider adding:**
  - an adjustable notan threshold slider (the threshold is the whole point of the exercise);
  - a 3/4/5-value posterize option;
  - a blur ("squint") toggle;
  - upside-down or 180° rotation (requested, and cheap);
  - grid density choices (3×3, 4×4, plus a thirds or diagonal option for design and CAD).
- **Zoom/crop and a colour picker** are expected in reference boards (PureRef) more than in timers. They're lower priority for timed sessions but useful for long studies.
- Per-image toggles that persist through a session (as with keyboard F/B/G) are the norm. Shortcuts should be single keys.

### Gaps
- No primary teaching source was retrieved that specifically recommends 3–5 value posterize, sight-size or grid-transfer method (e.g. atelier sources). This is common knowledge but uncited here.

## 4. End-of-session features, and the streak debate

### Takeaway
A summary listing every image shown, the count and total time (with the source credited) is the established pattern. Posemaniacs also keeps a 100-pose history. Streaks are contested: well-known artists and educators warn that "draw every day" pressure backfires, while some learners find streaks motivating.

### Cited Findings
- Art in Context shows an end summary: number of poses, total drawing time, and every pose with its source — [Art in Context](https://artincontext.org/pose-reference/)
- The Quick Poses iOS app shows a progress summary at the end of a session — [App Store: Quick Poses](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1510309896)
- Posemaniacs saves up to 100 poses of history and tells users to "review your drawings afterwards" — [Posemaniacs](https://www.posemaniacs.com/thirtysecond)
- Proko users asked to see the total session length *before* starting — [Proko forum](https://proko.com/community/topics/timer-tool-improvements)
- QuickPoses offers a "QP certificate" — [quickposes.com](https://quickposes.com/)
- **Streak debate:**
  - Loish (a professional illustrator) says daily drawing isn't sustainable for long periods and recommends drawing *regularly* — [loish.net: Drawing every day](https://loish.net/?p=1098)
  - Another guide warns that if you "make it about perfection, you will eventually rebel against your own rules" — [Chris Wilson Studio](https://chriswilsonstudio.com/how-to-draw-everyday/)
  - An essay questions streak culture's room for rest — [Substack: myth of the creative streak](https://shinjinim.substack.com/p/the-myth-of-maintaining-a-creative)
  - Burnout is described as output or pressure outpacing recovery — [Canvaslot](https://blog.canvaslot.com/?p=2758)
  - On the other side, an r/learnart commenter says "a streak helps me stick with it" — [r/learnart mirror](https://cal1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/learnart/comments/84tzy6/discussion_good_artistic_practices/dwc0ya7/?context=3)
  - Another writer says it "feels way easier to maintain a streak when I don't try to 'keep a streak'" — [Mirrored World](https://mirroredworld.bearblog.dev/stop-and-start/)

### Inferences
- LeMuse should end each session with a **contact sheet** of every image shown, with attribution and source link. This also satisfies ADR attribution rules. It should carry a count and total time, plus one-click Save or Favourite per image. It must still stream from the CDN; nothing is cached.
- History and stats should be **opt-in and pressure-free**: weekly totals rather than unbroken-chain streaks, no guilt notifications, and the ability to hide stats. That avoids the documented burnout concern while giving motivated users a record.

### Gaps
- No quantitative research was found on streaks in art practice specifically. The sources are blogs, a newsletter and forum posts.
- It's unconfirmed whether Line of Action or QuickPoses web show an end-of-session contact sheet.

## 5. Accessibility and comfort

### Takeaway
Users' main comfort asks are a **bigger timer**, an **optional "time almost up" sound**, **sound on/off**, **keyboard control**, a pause that is clearly distinct from "end", and distraction-free (black) backgrounds. Good tools also avoid stealing time: the timer starts on image load and pauses when the tab is hidden.

### Cited Findings
- Requests for a larger class-mode timer and an optional warning sound near the end (Feb 2024) — [LoA forum](https://line-of-action.com/forums/topic/what-would-you-like-to-see-in-the-next-version-of-line-of-action)
- Complaint that the timer is too small for poses of a minute or less — [LoA forum search summary](https://line-of-action.com/forums/topic/what-would-you-like-to-see-in-the-next-version-of-line-of-action)
- Art in Context's audio and focus behaviour:
  - three beeps in the last 3 s and a higher tone on pose change;
  - countdown starts after image load;
  - pauses when the tab is switched;
  - keyboard keys: Space, ←/→, F, B, G.

  — [Art in Context](https://artincontext.org/pose-reference/)
- Proko added keyboard controls (←/→, Space, Enter to restart the timer, "?" for help) after requests — [Proko forum](https://proko.com/community/topics/timer-tool-improvements)
- A "1 minute remaining" chime in a free gesture app — [itch.io Gesture App](https://bmswift.itch.io/gesture-app). A sound on/off toggle was added in PoseFlow — [PoseFlow devlog](https://onirou.itch.io/poseflow/devlog)
- The Quick Poses iOS app relabelled "end session" as "pause" to avoid accidental ends — [App Store: Quick Poses](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1510309896)
- Line of Action uses a black background "to reduce distractions" — [LoA article, 2011](https://line-of-action.com/article/completely-revamped-new-features)

### Inferences
LeMuse should offer:
- a large, optionally hideable timer, plus a subtle progress bar for peripheral vision;
- audio cues that are opt-in or easily muted, with separate "warning" and "next" tones. There should also be a visual-only alternative (screen-edge flash) for deaf or hard-of-hearing users and for quiet environments;
- a help overlay listing every shortcut;
- an "End session" behind confirmation, kept separate from Pause;
- auto-pause when the window loses focus;
- a timer that starts on image load.

### Gaps
- No formal accessibility review (WCAG-based) of any drawing timer was found.
- No user research on reduced-motion or colour-blind needs for these tools turned up.

## 6. Content safety: nudity filtering and minors

### Takeaway
The tools default to clothed content, with nudity behind an opt-in. Art in Context deliberately doesn't remember the choice, Proko labels packs, SketchDaily has a nudity toggle, and LoA has a "fully covered" classroom-suitable set. No tool documented age verification. Regulators (UK Online Safety Act child-safety duties, enforceable from 25 July 2025; US state age-verification laws) put pressure on sites that show nudity. Art commentators argue that this wrongly conflates artistic nudity with sexual content.

### Cited Findings
- "Include nude figure studies" is unticked by default, and that setting alone is **not saved between visits** — [Art in Context](https://artincontext.org/pose-reference/)
- SketchDaily's tool has nudity and gender options — [search summary of Proko community / guides](https://www.proko.com/community/topics/online-tools-for-practice); [doncorgi](https://doncorgi.com/blog/free-pose-reference-websites/)
- Proko packs are marked "contains nudity" or censored. A user requested a nude/clothed filter in the timer — [search summary, Proko community](https://www.proko.com/community/topics/online-tools-for-practice)
- Line of Action describes a "fully covered" standing-pose set "suitable for practice by anyone, including classroom usage". ### Inferences
- LeMuse is a desktop app that streams from public museum APIs and user-chosen Are.na channels. It doesn't host images, so the platform-duty regimes above are less directly relevant. But museum collections contain nudes (classical sculpture, life studies) and Are.na channels are unmoderated by LeMuse.
- A reasonable design would be:
  - a **"hide likely nudity" default** using source metadata where available (museum classifications and keywords);
  - an explicit opt-in for life-study topics, per the Art in Context pattern of not persisting the opt-in, or persisting it only per topic;
  - a clear statement that Are.na channel content is the user's choice.
- The tools found don't record subjects' ages. Practice in this field relies on curated, adult-model libraries. LeMuse can't guarantee that for third-party channels, so "report/hide this image" and "hide from this channel" actions matter.
