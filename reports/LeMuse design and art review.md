# Close the Practice Loop, Then Ship

LeMuse feels like "something is missing" mainly because it stops when the images appear. The job an artist hires it for is warming up, practising and seeing progress. That job has a beginning, a middle and an end, and until the practice-tools branch the app offered only an open stream. Theory explains why. Delight sits on top of a stack of functional, reliable, usable and pleasurable layers, and only counts once the lower layers hold. LeMuse's lower layers are strong: polite sync, stable error codes, offline states, attribution. The top layer is thin: nothing in the app says who it is for or reflects back what the user achieved. The new study aids and timed sessions close most of the gap. They already match the 2026 baseline of free timers like Art in Context's Pose Reference on presets, mirror, greyscale and grids, and they go further with value tones, balance and squint. What remains is mostly craft: a timer that starts only when the image has loaded and pauses when the window is hidden; a neutral, user-selectable surround; a museum-style label; images sharp enough for a 1440p screen; a calm voice and a story; and an end-of-session moment worth remembering. On the enterprise side, LeMuse already does more than many commercial reference apps: it keeps only CC0 Cleveland records, scopes in-app links, stores tokens in Credential Manager and sends no telemetry. Before 1.0 a solo maintainer still needs a consistent signing identity, published checksums with provenance, a one-page privacy statement, redacted bug reports, a trademark check on the name, and basic WCAG fixes. A large company would add SLSA-level pipelines, legal review of every source and formal accessibility audits. Those can wait, provided the deferral is written down.

## Why it feels unfinished: the job ends at "images appear"

The research converges on one diagnosis. Norman's model has three levels. The visceral level is first impact. The behavioural level is the feel of use. The reflective level is "the message it tells others about the owner's taste" ([Norman](https://jnd.org/emotional-design/)). Walter's hierarchy puts pleasure last, after function, reliability and usability, and notes that this top layer is "often missing in most products" ([vasilis.nl summary](https://vasilis.nl/research/2018/02/a-comparison-of-three-pyramids/)). Kathy Sierra's version is more pointed: nobody wants to be good at the app; they want to be good at what the app helps them do ([Business of Software](https://businessofsoftware.org/2012/11/kathy-sierra-building-the-minimum-badass-user/)). Jobs-to-be-done says the same thing in market terms: people "hire" a product for progress in a circumstance and "fire" it when the progress doesn't come ([Christensen Institute](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/jobs-to-be-done/)).

Seen that way, the gap is clear. A stream of attributed museum art is the *feature*. The job is "I sit down to draw and want to be warmed up and practising within a minute, without falling into a Pinterest hole." Before the practice tools, that job had no start, no structure and no end. Nothing in the UI showed that a session happened, how long it lasted or what was studied. The Peak-End rule, which says experiences are judged by their most intense moment and their ending ([Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/)), suggests why an endless slideshow leaves a flat memory, however good each image is.

The second cause is structural to solo work. Linear's engineers ship one small polish fix a week and reached more than **1,000 fixes in about two years**. Their stated reason: "It's hard to perceive subtle problems with a UI you yourself have built," and small fixes "together completely change how the product feels" ([Linear](https://linear.app/blog/quality-wednesdays)). A solo maker has no second pair of eyes by default. The cure is a ritual rather than heroics: a "quality" label, a weekly 30-minute polish slot, and the owner keeping a running "feels off" list during real drawing sessions.

The third cause is the missing story. Halide's features were not unique; its design and point of view were ([Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/2017/05/halide)). LeMuse has a strong, timely story that it barely tells. Artists are leaving Pinterest because it is "overrun with AI slop" and hand-drawn work gets auto-tagged as AI-modified ([404 Media](https://www.404media.co/pinterest-is-drowning-in-a-sea-of-ai-slop-and-auto-moderation/)). Cosmos built its whole pitch on being calm, ad-free and curated ([Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/design/social-media/what-is-cosmos-the-pinterest-alternative-for-creatives)). "Real museum art, free, no ads, no algorithm, always credit the artist" is now a selling point, not a neutral fact. Today it lives in ADRs rather than on the first screen, the About section or the README.

## Design Analyst: the stage, the feel and the first five minutes

**The surround behind the image is a perception instrument, not a theme colour.** Any grey can read as anything from black to white depending on its context ([Gilchrist, MIT](https://persci.mit.edu/gaz/publications/gazzan.dir/gazzan.htm)). A light surround darkens a dark region much more than a dark surround lightens a light one ([Frontiers 2014](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00640/full)). Viewing standards ask for a neutral, matte surround "without dominant colours", at about 10% of peak white ([ISO 12608 sample](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/1913/ff1979f9fabd411d8d87402d6b2d07b6/ISO-12608-1996.pdf)). Lightroom Classic lets users choose white, light grey, medium grey, dark grey or black behind the image ([Adobe Help](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom/help/setting-preferences-lightroom.html)). For LeMuse, the stage token should be **zero-chroma in every theme**: themes may tint the chrome, never the stage. The default should be a dark neutral grey rather than pure black, and a five-step surround picker belongs in View. Pure black makes the image the brightest thing in view, which inflates its perceived contrast compared with a print or a gallery wall. Apple's advice to dim images in Dark Mode ([Apple HIG](https://developer-mdn.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/foundations/dark-mode)) is wrong for an art viewer: the reference must never be altered without the user's say-so.

**Image sharpness is the most visible quality gap.** Cleveland's "web" image is only **900 px on the long side**, while "print" is 3,400 px ([CMA API docs](https://openaccess-api.clevelandart.org/)). The connector maps web to the `medium` role and print to `original`. The display order, large then medium then original, therefore shows the 900 px file full-window for every Cleveland slide, and it upscales visibly on a 1440p or 4K monitor. Choose by size: use print when the stage's device-pixel width exceeds about 1,000 px. That keeps preload ≤ 2 and costs bandwidth only on large windows. ADR-0007's "stream large first" intent already covers it.

**Feel is engineered with numbers.** Feedback within **0.1 s** feels caused by the user, within 1 s keeps flow, and past 10 s needs named progress and a Cancel ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/)). Windows motion tokens are 167/250/333 ms with `cubic-bezier(0,0,0,1)` on entry, 167 ms exits and 83 ms fades ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/signature-experiences/motion)). Next and previous should react at once with the caption, then fade the image in over 167–250 ms after `img.decode()`. That avoids a flash of empty stage. Opacity crossfades are not "motion" under WCAG 2.3.3, but slides and Ken Burns pans are ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)), so the existing `prefers-reduced-motion` rules should keep pans and zooms out of the stage entirely. Destructive actions such as removing a saved item or a topic should offer Undo rather than a warning, because people click through warnings by habit ([A List Apart](https://alistapart.com/article/neveruseawarning)). The app should also remember everything a returning user expects: last topic, position, window size and monitor, plan and study aids.

**The first five minutes should deliver art before setup.** NN/g's empty-state guidance has three rules. Say what the system is doing. Never show "nothing here" while content is still loading, which is "particularly harmful". Teach the next step in place, for example "Star your favorites to list them here" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/)). Progressive disclosure keeps advanced features behind a second level, and users who start with a limited core do better later ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)). The 15 starter topics are LeMuse's demo data. A first run should play one beautiful starter topic within seconds and offer a single gentle question ("What are you drawing today?") as topic chips. The Are.na token, Find channels and settings should stay out of the way until wanted. The empty Favourites view should say "Press ♥ on any image to keep it here."

**Identity needs one voice and one signature moment.** Mailchimp's rule is that the voice stays constant while the tone changes with the moment ([Mailchimp](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/)). LeMuse's voice could be calm, studio-like and knowledgeable, naming artists, with error copy such as "Cleveland didn't answer. Your saved images still work." The signature interaction is now obvious: a timed session with a quiet countdown ring that ends in a contact sheet. Polish that one flow hardest, and let the About section, README and first run tell the "real art, credited" story.

## Art Specialist: the practice tools meet the baseline; the details decide trust

The branch already covers most of what leading tools offer. Art in Context's free Pose Reference is the most complete free timer documented. It has class presets (10 × 30 s + 5 × 1 min, and up to a 1-hour ladder), custom steps, mirror, greyscale, 3 × 3/4 × 4 grids, back, pause, skip, beeps in the last 3 s and a summary listing every pose with its source ([Art in Context](https://artincontext.org/pose-reference/)). LeMuse's presets are gesture warm-up, relaxed, class (10 × 30 s, 5 × 1 min, 2 × 5 min, 1 × 10 min) and long study, plus a custom plan. Its class ladder matches the 30-minute class Line of Action users describe ([LoA forum](https://line-of-action.com/forums/topic/30-min-gesture-class)). The "Relaxed" preset answers a real complaint: one Proko learner started at 2 minutes because "the timer was making me feel pressured" ([Proko community](https://proko.com/community/topics/gesture-practice-1)).

| Capability | Leading tools | LeMuse branch | Gap to close |
|---|---|---|---|
| Class presets and custom plan | Art in Context, LoA, Quick Poses | Yes, 5 presets plus custom | Add a 1-hour class; show total length before start (Proko users asked) |
| Mirror, greyscale, grid | Art in Context, PureRef, GestureFlow | Yes, plus upside-down, thirds, diagonals | Persistent "Mirrored" badge so it is never forgotten |
| Notan / values | Proko notan lessons; Posemaniacs negative space | 2–5 tones with balance; squint blur | Label greyscale as luma; consider a negative-space view |
| Timer fairness | Art in Context starts the countdown on image load and pauses on tab switch | No visibility handling found in the working tree | Start on `decode()`, pause on hide or minimise |
| Back / restart pose | Art in Context, Proko (Enter restarts) | Pause, skip, end | Add back and restart-this-pose |
| Audio cues | Last-3-s beeps; "1 minute left" chime; sound toggle | Chime setting | Separate warning and next tones; a visual edge flash for quiet rooms |
| End of session | Summary with sources; Posemaniacs 100-pose history | Summary, keep as playlist | Make it a contact sheet with attribution and per-image ♥ |

**Timer fairness matters more for LeMuse than for its rivals**, because every image streams from a museum or Are.na CDN. A 3-second load would cost 10% of a 30-second pose. Art in Context starts its countdown only after the image loads and pauses when the user switches tabs ([Art in Context](https://artincontext.org/pose-reference/)). LeMuse should start the clock on `decode()` and pause on window hide, minimise or loss of focus. Line of Action users asked for a **larger timer** and an optional warning sound near the end ([LoA forum](https://line-of-action.com/forums/topic/what-would-you-like-to-see-in-the-next-version-of-line-of-action)). The Quick Poses iOS app replaced "End session" with "Pause" to stop accidental endings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1510309896)). End should therefore sit apart from Pause and behind a confirmation or an undo.

**Value tools must be honest about what they compute.** CSS `grayscale()` applies Rec.709 luma weights of 0.2126/0.7152/0.0722 to gamma-encoded sRGB ([W3C Filter Effects](https://www.w3.org/TR/filter-effects-1/)). That is close to perceived lightness for most images but diverges at high chroma ([Jacobson](https://alecjacobson.com/weblog/3157.html)), and a user study preferred a CIELAB-based conversion ([Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/a-psychological-study-importance-of-contrast-and-luminance-in-color-to-grayscale-mapping)). A saturated red and a mid-green can read as the same value in LeMuse's greyscale but differ to the eye. The display-only CSS/SVG approach is right: it reads no pixels, avoids canvas CORS tainting ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/How_to/CORS_enabled_image)) and keeps "only Save writes image bytes". The help text should still say "greyscale (luma), close to value, not exact for saturated colour." An exact L* or an automatic Otsu threshold would need pixel reads, so first check whether the CDNs send CORS headers. The balance slider is the best part of the notan view, because the threshold is the whole exercise ([Proko notan](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/project-notan-master-thumbnails/downloads)).

**The label should read like a museum wall, not a database row.** The tombstone order is artist (with nationality and dates), title, date, medium, dimensions and credit line ([Akron Art Museum](https://akronartmuseum.org/media/whats-in-a-label/); [Menil guidelines](https://d27m4mjhi8p0i4.cloudfront.net/api/file/Gt0Lg4fXSR60YPZIGKFo)). Cleveland exposes `measurements`, `creditline` and `share_license_status` ([CMA API](https://openaccess-api.clevelandart.org/)), but the connector keeps only type, technique and creation date. The label should put the creator in a stronger weight and the title in italics, then the date, then medium and dimensions muted, then a credit line with a **CC0 badge** for museum works. Are.na blocks should get a distinct "rights belong to the creator; for personal reference" line. Dimensions also serve the CAD and industrial-design audience, which reference feeds underserve. Showing all views of one museum object together would serve them the same way.

**Content safety and motivation need defaults, not features.** Timers hide nudity unless the user opts in, and Art in Context deliberately does not save that opt-in ([Art in Context](https://artincontext.org/pose-reference/)). Museum collections contain classical nudes, and LeMuse does not moderate Are.na channels. A "hide image" and "hide this channel" action is proportionate. A metadata-based nudity filter belongs on the roadmap. On streaks, intact streaks raise engagement, but broken ones push people away unless the streak can be repaired ([CU Boulder on Silverman & Barasch](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)). Working illustrators argue for drawing regularly rather than daily ([Loish](https://loish.net/?p=1098)). Any history should be a forgiving log ("12 days this month, 9 artists"), never a chain to break, and it should be hideable.

**Colour accuracy can't be promised, so say so.** Windows 11 Auto Color Management maps non-colour-aware apps into sRGB ([Microsoft DirectX blog](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/directx/auto-color-management)). Chromium-based browsers have reported problems with wide-gamut and multi-monitor setups ([Mozilla bug 1882239](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1882239)), and WebView2 can't set the flags that fix them ([LINQPad forum](https://forum.linqpad.net/discussion/comment/7618)). Museum JPEGs are normally sRGB and display as intended on an sRGB-calibrated screen. One help paragraph saying that, plus "no filters touch the image unless you turn on a study aid", is the honest stance.

## What a large company would handle, and what a solo project should

LeMuse is already ahead of most reference apps on the hardest items. The Cleveland connector requests `cc0=1` and drops any record whose `share_license_status` isn't CC0. That matters because Cleveland returns non-CC0 records whose images aren't open access ([CMA Open Access](https://clevelandart.org/open-access)). The Tauri opener is scoped to Are.na, Cleveland and the project's repo rather than the broad default, which allows mailto, tel and any http(s) URL ([Tauri opener](https://v2.tauri.app/plugin/opener/)). SECURITY.md, CONTRIBUTING and issue forms exist, and there is no telemetry. The table below sorts the rest by proportionality.

| Concern | What a major company does | Proportionate for a solo MIT project | Defer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Code signing | EV/OV cert in an HSM, Store listing | One consistent identity. EV **no longer bypasses SmartScreen since 2024**; OV costs $150–300/yr; Azure Artifact Signing (~$9.99/mo) is open to **individuals only in the USA and Canada** ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/package-and-deploy/code-signing-options)). SignPath Foundation is free, but it requires a prior release, CI builds, MFA and a code-signing-policy page, and it names itself as publisher ([SignPath terms](https://signpath.org/terms)) | MSIX Store packaging |
| Release integrity | SLSA L3, reproducible builds, SBOMs | `SHA256SUMS`, `actions/attest` provenance, a CycloneDX SBOM ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/security-for-github-actions/using-artifact-attestations/using-artifact-attestations-to-establish-provenance-for-builds); [cargo-cyclonedx](https://github.com/CycloneDX/cyclonedx-rust-cargo)); these meet Scorecard's Signed-Releases and SBOM checks ([Scorecard](https://github.com/ossf/scorecard/blob/main/docs/checks.md)) | Reproducible NSIS builds |
| Auto-update | Signed update channel, staged rollout | If added, the Tauri updater signature can't be disabled, and **losing the key strands every install** ([Tauri updater](https://v2.tauri.app/plugin/updater/)). Keep the key in Actions secrets only, never in an agent's hands (Microsoft says the same) | Staged rollout |
| Privacy | DPO, policy, consent flows | A one-page statement: no telemetry, which hosts are contacted, token stored in Credential Manager, everything else local. With no data reaching the developer, controller status is unlikely ([Ashfords on ICO](https://www.ashfords.co.uk/insights/articles/information-commissioners-office-highlights-the-importance-of-privacy-in-the-design-and-development-of-mobile-applications)); CCPA thresholds start at ~$26.6M revenue ([Clym](https://www.clym.io/blog/ccpa-applicability-guide)) | Legal review |
| "Report a problem" | Redacting crash pipeline | Redact `C:\Users\<name>`, tokens and emails; show the text before opening; warn "this will be public"; keep the URL short to avoid HTTP 414 ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/issues/tracking-your-work-with-issues/using-issues/creating-an-issue#creating-an-issue-from-a-url-query)). Are.na tokens never expire ([Are.na docs](https://www.are.na/developers/all.md)), so a leaked one stays valid | Crash upload (would make the owner a GDPR controller) |
| Copyright | Licensing team, takedown desk | CC0 badge; "rights belong to the creator" on Are.na items; a sidecar with source, creator and licence on Save; a README takedown section pointing to Are.na's DMCA agent ([Are.na Terms](https://are.na/terms)); no bulk save. Hotlinking is generally not direct infringement under the US server test ([Studicata](https://www.studicata.com/categories/intellectual-property-media-and-technology/inline-linking-and-the-server-test)), and the EU's *BestWater* ruling treats framing similarly ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright_aspects_of_hyperlinking_and_framing)); Save is the riskier act | Per-jurisdiction opinions |
| API terms | Partner agreements | Already stricter than Are.na's guidance (lazy pagination, a UA with contact details, backing off on 429) ([Are.na docs](https://www.are.na/developers/all.md)). Add a link to revoke tokens and a read-only scope hint | Asking Are.na about Save (owner's call) |
| Trademark | Clearance searches in every market | Use plain-text source names with an "unaffiliated" line, which is nominative use ([Playboy v. Welles](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/279/796/506072)). Search "LeMuse" in Nice classes 9 and 42; "Muse" is a crowded mark | Registration |
| In-app GitHub links | Links routed through a company domain | Never rename or delete the repo or account: GitHub's redirect **stops working once someone creates a repo with the old name** ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/creating-and-managing-repositories/renaming-a-repository)), and shipped binaries keep their old links forever. Build issue URLs with `URLSearchParams`. Drop `artic.edu` from the opener list while AIC is held back | Custom domain redirect |
| Accessibility | WCAG audit, VPAT | The EAA likely doesn't apply to free, non-commercial software ([EUR-Lex 2019/882](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj/eng)). Do the WCAG basics anyway: 24 px targets ([W3C 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)), pause for auto-advance (2.2.2), a way to turn off single-key shortcuts (2.1.4), and alt text from title, creator and date. Today the alt text is the title only | Formal audit |
| Governance | CLA, code owners, two reviewers | A CODE_OF_CONDUCT (missing), DCO rather than a CLA, the Scorecard Action, and generated THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES ([cargo-about](https://github.com/EmbarkStudios/cargo-about)). Scorecard's Code-Review check can't pass for a solo maintainer, because "Bot or AI reviews do not count"; record that rather than game it | Second maintainer |

Two caveats apply. This is research, not legal advice, and several legal sources are secondary or older (the US server test case dates from 2007, Are.na's Terms from 2023). Whether saving a copy for personal study is lawful also depends on the user's country, so the app should frame Save as personal reference and never suggest that non-CC0 images are free to reuse.

## Conclusion

The main finding is that LeMuse's missing piece was never another source or setting. It was the drawing session itself, and the branch now under review is the first change aimed at the user's job rather than at the plumbing. That changes how 1.0 should be judged. The bar is no longer "does every feature work", which it largely does. It is whether a ten-minute warm-up feels fair, calm and complete from first frame to contact sheet. Because the field's leaders are free web timers and closed-source boards, a well-crafted open-source Windows app that combines timed practice, honest value tools and credited, CC0-first museum sources would have little direct competition. Craft and story will win that position more than features will.

The enterprise question has a more reassuring answer than expected. The costly items a big company worries about, such as source licensing, link scope, token storage and telemetry, are already handled by design. What remains for a solo project is mostly process: one signing identity kept forever, provenance attached to each release, redaction before anything goes public, and a written list of what was deliberately deferred. The sources leave three things open: whether the image CDNs send CORS headers (which decides if exact L* and auto-threshold are possible), how WebView2 actually renders wide-gamut images on Windows 11, and whether "LeMuse" is clear as a mark. Each should be tested or searched, not assumed.
