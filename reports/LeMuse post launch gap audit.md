# Give LeMuse Zoom, a Tray, Folders

The "something missing" is a feature, not a theme, and the clearest single candidate is **zoom and pan to a detail**. Every reference board has it, timer apps are adding it in 2026, and LeMuse still can't do it. LeMuse already meets or beats the 2026 baseline for timed practice. Line of Action's 2025 rebuild set that baseline: a clock that waits for the image, warning sounds, a big timer and a review after the session, and LeMuse has all of it. Where it falls short is on the board and library side, which artists also expect: looking closely at a detail, drawing from their own folders, and handing an image to Krita or PureRef. On the desktop side, an app built to run in the background still disappears when its window closes: there is no tray icon and no "start with Windows". Two more causes are close to the surface. The release paperwork lags: the app still calls itself 1.0.0 after two features shipped, and there is no "What's new". The themes don't follow Windows and can't be shared. Neither of those needs more colour palettes. The recommended order is: **1.1** adds zoom, a memory-drawing mode, a tray and themes that follow Windows, plus the release tidy-up. **1.2** adds your own folders, provenance on Save, drag-out to art apps, and library backup, all under one reviewed rule that "a user's export counts as a Save". **1.3** adds two cheap museum sources (SMK and The Met), measuring overlays and a colour picker, then the auto-updater. None of this breaks stream-first, adds image caches, works around bot protection or widens a permission without the owner's review.

## Internal state: the code is clean, the paperwork is two features behind

LeMuse's engineering health on `main` (commit 92e34c3, checked 2026-11-21) is good. **Every check passes**: cargo fmt, clippy with warnings as errors, **125 Rust tests**, the npm type-check, **23 UI unit tests**, the npm audit gate and cargo audit. Cargo audit has six allowed warnings, all in Linux-only GTK/zbus crates plus a "chrono unmaintained" notice, none shipped on Windows. There are no TODO or FIXME markers in the source. Two sources are live: Are.na and Cleveland. The AIC connector is built but held back behind its Cloudflare challenge ([audiences](../docs/product/audiences.md)). The permission set is still narrow: window controls, plus opener links scoped to Are.na, Cleveland and the project's own repository. The asset protocol is limited to `$PICTURE/LeMuse/**` ([capabilities](../app/src-tauri/capabilities/default.json)). The coordinator's audit confirmed that none of these exist in code yet: zoom/pan, compare, local folders, a Save provenance sidecar, tray, start with Windows, daily set, avoid repeats, colour palette, auto-updater, drag-out, library backup/restore and image notes. LeMuse does already back up its catalog before every schema upgrade, and it already beeps three times before a pose ends ([CHANGELOG](../CHANGELOG.md)). The 2026 "warning sound" baseline is therefore met.

The weak spot is the release and documentation trail. **Tag v1.0.0 is on origin, but `main` carries two merged features after it**: the calmer bar (#5) and custom themes with Pine & coral (#6). The version is still 1.0.0 everywhere, and the CHANGELOG's "Unreleased" section mixes 1.0 items with post-1.0 items. The next release, 1.1.0, needs a version bump and a changelog split. The owner's portable copy was briefly stale because the portable folder wasn't rebuilt after themes landed. Under the project's rule that "every miss becomes a check", the portable build should become a release-workflow asset with a hash check against the release build. [PLAN](../docs/PLAN.md) has drifted too. "Where we are" still says v0.7.0. The Roadmap lists as open many items that shipped in #20–#22: grid view, search, the Saved view, renaming topics, the practice log, the Are.na token and museum labels. Deferred finding **D-42** (the picomatch audit exception) is still listed, but the exceptions file is empty, so it's closed. **A-50**, the CI actions pinned to Node 20 releases, is still open. The latest PROGRESS entry still says "awaiting owner review", though #6 is merged.

Two items from the original "Path to 1.0" were never built. Item 6 is local folders as a source plus hiding a whole channel (DA-15). Item 7 is the desktop finish: tray, start with Windows, `lemuse backup`, and the Save provenance sidecar. The roadmap, in other words, already names most of what this audit finds missing. Several owner questions are still open: Q18 and Q21 (web hosting), Q22 (the mailer account) and Q23 (contacting Are.na). This matters for the diagnosis. An owner who ships two features and then sees the app still say 1.0.0, with no "What's new" to point at, will feel that something is unfinished even when the code is fine.

## Feature or theme? A feature (zoom), plus themes that don't follow Windows

The direct answer is **a feature, and specifically zoom**. Among the tools researched, the gap that recurs across unrelated apps is looking closer. GestureFlow, a paid Windows gesture tool, ships zoom, pan, a custom grid and mirror ([Cubebrush](https://cubebrush.co/alwayshungry/products/ae75sq/gestureflow-gesture-drawing-reference-study)). The itch.io "Gesture App" added "zoom & pan on image" in its v2 ([itch.io](https://bmswift.itch.io/gesture-app)). Draw Gestures' Android reviewers asked for zoom, and the developer replied "We are working on the zoom feature" ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.darkmat13r.gesturedrawing&hl=en)). BeeRef's last release added scroll-wheel panning ([BeeRef releases](https://github.com/rbreu/beeref/releases)). LeMuse already streams Cleveland's 3,400 px print images on large windows, but there's no way to look into them. A CAD or product designer can't read a joint or a hinge. A figure artist can't study a hand. The second recurring ask is **"use my own images"**. DrawGestures offers offline local folders free and charges for cloud imports ([DrawGestures](https://drawgestures.com/guides/free-gesture-drawing-app-and-website)), and Line of Action now lets members draw only from their own playlists ([Line of Action](https://line-of-action.com/article/a-new-version-of-the-tools-is-now-online)). The evidence for these is qualitative: forum posts, store reviews and changelogs. No vote counts or surveys were found, and Reddit couldn't be retrieved. Even so, zoom and own-folder pools are the two asks that surfaced independently in several tools.

The theme half of the feeling is real, but it isn't a missing palette. LeMuse ships **six built-in themes** (Studio, Gallery, Blueprint, Paper, Millennium, Pine & coral) and lets users make their own ([settings.ts](../app/src/settings.ts)). That is already more colour choice than Photoshop's four interface greys or Krita's five "looks" ([Krita docs](https://docs.krita.org/Settings_Menu)). Apps that feel complete treat theming as a loop of make, share and install. Blender installs and saves XML theme files ([Blender Manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/de/4.5/editors/preferences/themes.html)). Obsidian's directory of plugins and themes "more than doubled to over 8,000 projects" after its 2026 relaunch ([Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/blog/2026-obsidian-october/)). Krita's docs admit there is "no easy way to create and share themes", and its users notice ([Krita docs](https://docs.krita.org/Settings_Menu)). No study links theme ecosystems to perceived completeness, so this is an inference. It points to three cheap fixes. "Auto" should switch between a light and a dark theme the user picks, following Windows. A theme should export and import as a short string or file. And the theme editor should say plainly why the stage behind the image always stays neutral grey. That rule is LeMuse's best art-viewing decision, and right now nobody sees it.

There is a third, quieter cause: the app has **no presence on the desktop**. LeMuse is built to run in the background, with a mini player, auto refresh and a slideshow. Yet closing the window ends it, it can't start with Windows, and its releases don't announce themselves. A tray icon, an opt-in start-up toggle and a bundled "What's new" panel are each a day or less of work ([Tauri tray](https://v2.tauri.app/learn/system-tray/); [Tauri autostart](https://v2.tauri.app/plugin/autostart/)). Together they change how "finished" the app feels more than any single new tool would.

## What the 2026 field has that LeMuse doesn't

### Practice tools: memory drawing and measuring are open ground

Timed practice is saturated, so the gains are elsewhere. **Memory drawing** (study, hide, draw, compare) is an established teaching method. Horace Lecoq de Boisbaudran taught it in Paris to students including Rodin and Fantin-Latour ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Lecoq_de_Boisbaudran)), and Proko still teaches a "Visual Memory Games" project ([Proko](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/project-visual-memory-games)). Cognitive scientists use drawing from memory as a precise probe of what a person actually took in. In one study, participants who drew 30 studied scenes from memory produced drawings detailed enough for raters to match each one to its image ([Bainbridge et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/6315028)). Yet **no major timer ships an automatic show-then-hide mode**. The closest tool found is DrawRef on iPad, where you hide the reference by hand ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/drawref/id1446586198)). LeMuse's session engine already holds the image in memory, so a "Look → Draw (hidden) → Compare" step type is cheap. It needs no new permission and no storage. It should be a separate preset, not the default, because Lecoq himself advised starting memory work only after some skill from observation. One caution: the research uses memory drawing to *measure* memory. No controlled study shows that memory-drawing training improves observational accuracy, so the app shouldn't claim it does.

**Measuring overlays** come next. Comparative measurement means taking one part of the subject (a head, a window bay) as a unit and checking other lengths and angles against it. It is a core atelier method ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_measurement)), and GestureFlow sells its Proportion Tool as a signature feature. PureRef 2.1 added only shapes, lines and a snapping grid ([CGPress](https://cgpress.org/archives/pureref-2-1-adds-shapes-grids-and-language-support.html)). LeMuse could offer plumb and level lines, an angle readout, a proportion ruler ("2.4 heads") and a bounding-box ratio. All of them can be drawn in SVG over the image, so they never read a pixel. To answer the over-tooling worry, they should default to "hold to show": draw first, then check. Ctrl+Paint's view is that grids help beginners "truly draw what they're looking at" but are not "a good way to learn drawing technique" if leaned on forever ([Ctrl+Paint](https://www.ctrlpaint.com/blog/on-cheating-rulers-grids-and-tracing)).

**Colour** is where the stream-first rule bites. Reading pixels from a canvas fails when the image host sends no CORS header, because the canvas becomes "tainted" ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/CORS_Enabled_Image)). A local test on 2026-11-21 found that Cleveland's CDN sends none, so automatic palette extraction would fail on half of LeMuse's sources. The workaround is the browser's **EyeDropper API**. The user clicks, picks a colour anywhere on screen, and no pixel data is available without that action ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/EyeDropper_API)). LeMuse can then show the value (L\*), hue and a warm/cool label, and keep a hand-built five-swatch strip stored as hex strings, never as image data. Whether EyeDropper works inside LeMuse's WebView2 origin is unverified, so test it before committing. For designers, a **perspective finder** fits the curricula that ID students follow, such as Scott Robertson's boxes-and-grids foundation ([Gnomon](https://instructors.thegnomonworkshop.com/workshops/basic-perspective-form-drawing)). The user drags two pairs of converging edges, LeMuse finds the vanishing points and draws the horizon. It's more geometry and UX work, so it comes later. On motivation, the earlier decision against streaks holds up. In one study, points, levels and leaderboards produced no measurable change in intrinsic motivation ([Mekler et al. 2017](https://glassmanlab.seas.harvard.edu/annotated_works/mekler17.pdf)). Progress the user can see in their own work, such as a "draw it again in a week" revisit, is the better lever, though its evidence is indirect.

### Browsing and your own images: table stakes LeMuse skips

Board tools set the expectations LeMuse half-meets: zoom/pan, local files, always on top, greyscale, a colour sampler and notes. PureRef also offers "always on top of a single application" and "transparent to mouse" ([PureRef handbook](https://www.pureref.com/handbook/features/)). The mini player covers plain always-on-top. Zoom and local folders are the big gaps. For local folders, the model to copy is Allusion's: watched folders are read in place and never copied ([Allusion](https://allusion-app.github.io)). That fits "only Save writes image bytes" exactly. The Windows trap is OneDrive Files On-Demand. Opening a cloud-only placeholder downloads it, so the folder scanner must check the `FILE_ATTRIBUTE_RECALL_ON_DATA_ACCESS` attribute first and skip such files ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/drivers/ifs/placeholders_guidance)). Eagle's auto-import supports "only local paths, not network or NAS" ([Eagle](https://en.eagle.cool/support/article/the-auto-import)), which suggests warning on network paths rather than promising them. Edge and WebView2 can't render HEIC ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/HEIC)), so phone photos need a "format not supported" card rather than a broken image.

A full **board or canvas** is the one table stake LeMuse should not chase. It is a separate product category. The better move is to make LeMuse a good partner to PureRef and BeeRef through drag-out (next section). Comparing two to four images side by side is a smaller, reasonable later item.

### Desktop and ambient use: tray first, widgets not for now

For an unpackaged, unsigned build, the integrations worth their cost are clear. A **tray icon** is a core Tauri feature, not a plugin. Its menu can carry Show, Pause/Play, Next, Mini player, Refresh and Quit, and closing the window can then mean "keep playing" ([Tauri tray](https://v2.tauri.app/learn/system-tray/)). **Start with Windows** is an official plugin with three narrow permissions. It writes the per-user `Run` key in the registry ([Tauri autostart](https://v2.tauri.app/plugin/autostart/)). It should be off by default, launch minimised, and be hidden in the portable build, where a moved folder would leave a dead registry entry. One practical point: the plugin needs **Rust 1.90**, and the workspace currently declares 1.89.

**Drag-out** is the most "integrated" feature for an art-reference app: drag the current image straight into a Krita canvas, PureRef or Blender. The community `tauri-plugin-drag` from CrabNebula (2.1.1, May 2026, about 40,000 downloads a month) does this ([lib.rs](https://lib.rs/crates/tauri-plugin-drag); [drag-rs](https://github.com/crabnebula-dev/drag-rs)). It needs a file on disk, so a drag must count as a Save. Windows adds a pitfall: Tauri's native drop handler and HTML5 drag-and-drop can't both run, so the drag has to be prototyped first ([wyhinton](https://wyhinton.github.io/2026/01/14/Tauri-DragAndDrop-Issues)). Nobody has tested whether Krita, PureRef and Blender accept the drop, so a manual check comes first. "Open saved file" and "Show in folder" use the existing opener plugin. Its `allow-open-path` permission is not in the default set and should stay out of the WebView: a Rust command that takes only a block ID should resolve the path ([Tauri opener](https://v2.tauri.app/plugin/opener/)).

**Provenance on Save** is the highest-trust item for this audience. Artists keep references for years, and attribution is a founding LeMuse rule. The plan is to embed a few IPTC/XMP fields in saved JPEGs and write a JSON sidecar for every format. The JPEG fields are Creator, Credit Line, rights, source, and the depicted artwork's title and inventory URL ([IPTC 2025.1](http://www.iptc.org/std/photometadata/specification/IPTC-PhotoMetadata-2025.1.html)). Windows Explorer's "Authors" field reads `dc:creator` from XMP first ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/el-gr/windows/win32/wic/-wic-photoprop-system-author)). Lightroom writes XMP into JPEG, TIFF, PSD and DNG but doesn't list PNG ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom/help/metadata-basics-actions.html)), which is why the sidecar is needed. Embedding changes the file's bytes, and that would break the content-addressed `objects/<sha256>` store. **Recommendation:** keep the stored original byte-exact, and embed only in the `views/` copy or a "Save as" copy. Check whether Eagle reads the embedded fields; no source confirms it. **Backup/restore** completes the trust story. SQLite's `VACUUM INTO` writes a consistent single-file snapshot ([SQLite](https://www.sqlite.org/lang_vacuum.html)), and PowerToys now backs up its config before every update ([PowerToys notes](https://newreleases.io/project/github/microsoft/PowerToys/release/v0.99.0)). A `lemuse backup` file holds the catalog snapshot, `lemuse.toml` and a manifest, and it has to be added to `.gitignore` and `FORBIDDEN_FILES`. Pair it with Krita-style "Copy diagnostic info", which redacts Windows user paths and tokens ([Krita docs](https://docs.krita.org/Help_Menu)).

The **auto-updater** should come after backup. It works with NSIS and a static `latest.json`, but its signature can't be switched off, and losing the key strands every install ([Tauri updater](https://v2.tauri.app/plugin/updater/)). It must be disabled in the portable build, which it would otherwise convert into an installed copy. **winget** has no Authenticode requirement on its submission page. It needs a direct HTTPS URL, a silent install and uninstall, and clean antivirus scans ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/package-manager/package/repository)). That makes it the cheapest discovery channel, but it is the owner's call because it publishes under the owner's name. **Desktop wallpaper** from *saved* images is feasible through the plain Win32 `IDesktopWallpaper` API, including Windows' own slideshow over a folder ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/api/shobjidl_core/nn-shobjidl_core-idesktopwallpaper)), so it is a good later item. **Widgets** need a packaged (MSIX) app ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/develop/widgets/widget-providers)). Toast notifications show PowerShell's name in unpackaged builds and have no action buttons on Windows ([Tauri notification](https://v2.tauri.app/plugin/notification/)). Deep links arrive as untrusted command-line text ([Tauri deep linking](https://v2.tauri.app/plugin/deep-linking/)). Jump lists have no Tauri plugin, and the lock screen is unproven outside UWP. All of these should be deferred. The Microsoft Store is now free for individuals ([Windows Developer Blog](https://blogs.windows.com/windowsdeveloper/2025/09/10/free-developer-registration-for-individual-developers-on-microsoft-store/)). For an unsigned project the only route is MSIX, which Microsoft signs ([winapp CLI](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/dev-tools/winapp-cli/guides/tauri)). That is a later owner decision, and it would also unlock widgets and toasts.

### Sources: SMK and The Met are the cheap next museums

The best next source matches the Cleveland connector's shape: one keyword call that returns open-licence records with images, served from one fixed image host the CSP can allow-list. **SMK** (Denmark) fits exactly. Its `art/search` call takes `keys` plus `has_image` and `public_domain` filters and returns full records with IIIF images, and public-domain works "including photos, can be used freely" ([SMK](https://smk.dk/en/article/smk-api)). **The Met** is the biggest keyless CC0 set, but it changed in 2026. The old search "will be retired on October 1, 2026". The new `v1.1/search` takes `offset` and `limit` but still returns object IDs only, so each keyword costs one search plus up to N object calls. Images appear only for open-access objects ([Met API](https://metmuseum.github.io/)). Capping N at about 24 keeps the weekly cost small at LeMuse's polite pacing. Its decorative-arts, arms-and-armour and furniture departments also suit the object-heavy starter topics. **Wikimedia Commons** fills the architecture, machine and tool gap best. It is harder: licences vary per file and the CC BY-SA ones carry attribution duties, and since early 2026 non-standard thumbnail widths are "rate-limited strictly", so LeMuse must request standard widths such as 960, 1280 or 1920 ([MediaWiki](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Common_thumbnail_sizes)). **Smithsonian Open Access** is CC0 and needs a free api.data.gov key, with a default limit of 1,000 requests an hour ([api.data.gov](https://api.data.gov/docs/developer-manual)). That key belongs in Credential Manager. Rijksmuseum's new keyless search has no free-text keyword field ([Rijksmuseum](https://data.rijksmuseum.nl/docs/search)), so it ranks lower.

Several sources should be refused. Harvard's API is "for non-commercial use only" and forbids storing content "for more than two weeks" ([Harvard](https://github.com/harvardartmuseums/api-docs)), which clashes with Save. Science Museum Group's images are CC BY-NC-SA, and it warns that harvesting from its IIIF/Zoom endpoints gets access blocked ([SMG](https://coimages.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/datasets/index.html)). The Library of Congress can serve "HTML CAPTCHA pages even below the listed rates" ([LoC](https://www.loc.gov/apis/json-and-yaml/working-within-limits/)). Any connector there must treat an HTML response as a hard stop, never something to solve. Aggregators such as Openverse and Europeana serve images from many hosts that a per-host CSP can't allow-list. None of the shortlisted image hosts were probed for bot challenges or CORS. Before each connector, the owner should open a sample image once in a normal browser, which is the check that caught AIC.

### Themes: build the loop, protect the stage

The theme work that would feel like completion is small. First, **Auto** that pairs a chosen light theme with a chosen dark theme and follows Windows. Second, a **share string and file**, validated strictly: four `#rrggbb` values and a capped name rendered as text, never CSS or URLs. Third, real support for Windows contrast themes through `forced-colors`, which maps the chrome to the user's system palette ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/fluent-ui/web-components/design-system/high-contrast)). Themes should be keyed by a stable ID, not by name; Obsidian users hit name collisions and asked for exactly that ([Obsidian forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/theme-name-collisions-with-conflicting-download-counts-suggestion-theme-ids/117014.md)). Following the Windows accent colour is optional and later. Chromium's `AccentColor` is limited to installed web apps and isn't confirmed shipped ([blink-dev](https://groups.google.com/a/chromium.org/g/blink-dev/c/knAC85FErrE)), so the accent would have to be read in Rust.

If more built-in themes are wanted, a small **Studio pack** with real stories beats a long list. "Museum grey" would follow the Rijksmuseum's 2013 move from white walls to grey ([Architect Magazine](https://www.architectmagazine.com/design/buildings/rijksmuseum-renovation_o/)). "Safelight" would be a night theme in darkroom amber. Kodak's and Ilford's filters define it by wavelength, not hex, so it would be an approximation ([Ilford](https://www.ilfordphoto.com/amfile/file/download/file/605/product/613)). "Graphite & newsprint" would be a greyscale-first theme for value study. Community palettes such as Catppuccin are MIT-licensed ([Catppuccin](https://github.com/catppuccin/catppuccin)) and can be credited by name, but they are tuned for code editors, so their colour belongs in chrome and accent only. Pantone asserts its colour names: "the trademark PANTONE must always precede each PANTONE Color name", and cross-referencing needs written permission ([Pantone guidelines](https://pantone.com.br/mur_general_trademark.pdf)). No theme should be called "Cloud Dancer" or "Colour of the Year". The 2026 colour is Cloud Dancer ([Pantone](https://www.pantone.com/articles/press-releases/pantone-announces-color-of-the-year-2026-cloud-dancer)).

Four ideas should be refused, with the reasons recorded in the ADR. An **ambient glow sampled from the image** tints the surround with the image's own hue, which shifts how the image's colours are seen through simultaneous contrast, and it would need pixel reads. **Mica or Acrylic behind the stage** tints it with the wallpaper. Tauri also documents Acrylic lag on resize ([Tauri window API](https://v2.tauri.app/reference/javascript/api/namespacewindow/)). A **"dyslexia font" sold as an accessibility fix** has no support: OpenDyslexic showed "no improvement in reading rate or accuracy" ([Wery & Diliberto](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5629233/)), and a 2026 meta-analysis found no consistent benefit from such fonts ([Azzarello et al.](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/97732/1/Azzarello-etal-AD-2026-Does-font-improve-reading-in-dyslexic-children-meta-analysis.pdf)). A legible font plus spacing controls is the honest option. And **seasonal themes** add novelty with no artistic value.

## Next releases: 1.1 looks closer, 1.2 owns the files, 1.3 widens the world

The order follows three rules. First, ship the items that need no new permission. Second, group everything that writes image bytes to disk under **one ADR amendment that defines "a user-initiated export counts as a Save"**, so the owner reviews LeMuse's core privacy promise once, not four times. Third, add new sources only after provenance on Save exists, so every new museum's saved files carry their licence from the first day. Each line is one branch and one PR, merged before the next starts. Every new Cargo feature, plugin, permission or CSP host is listed for the owner's `guardrails-reviewed` label, and every new dependency must clear the 14-day cooldown. `tauri-plugin-autostart` 2.5.1 and `tauri-plugin-drag` 2.1.1 both already do.

After 1.3, the queue in rough order is Wikimedia Commons, then Smithsonian (key in Credential Manager), then Poly Haven. Next come the perspective finder, a "draw it again" revisit queue and a weekly fixed set, a self-review overlay of a photo of your drawing, and the Studio theme pack. Self-review needs a privacy review: the photo stays in memory only, and EXIF is stripped on any save. Saved images as a Windows wallpaper slideshow and side-by-side compare come last. The parked list is a board or canvas, toasts, deep links, jump lists, widgets, the lock screen, a screensaver, Store MSIX, automatic palette extraction, camera capture, streaks, and the Harvard, Science Museum Group, V&A, LoC and aggregator sources.

## Owner decisions that can't be cheaply reversed

| Decision | Why it's hard to undo | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **"A user-initiated export counts as a Save"** (drag-out, Open with, wallpaper, embedded metadata) | It redefines the "no images except on Save" promise that users and the privacy statement rely on | Approve it as one ADR amendment that lists each gesture, so the promise stays auditable |
| **Stored original kept byte-exact vs embedded metadata** | Once files are written with one hashing rule, changing it means migrating every saved file | Keep `objects/` byte-exact; embed XMP only in `views/` and Save-as copies; always write the JSON sidecar |
| **Local folders in the asset scope** | It widens what the WebView may read on disk; a loose scope is a lasting security exposure | Per-folder runtime scope chosen in a dialog, never a whole drive or user profile; no network paths |
| **Auto-updater signing key** | Losing the key strands every installed copy; a leaked key lets anyone push updates | Ship it only after backup exists; key in Actions secrets with an offline copy only the owner holds |
| **Publishing to winget (and later the Store)** | Listings are public, under the owner's identity, and outlive the app's choices | Do winget after 1.1 ships; park the Store until MSIX is worth a second pipeline |
| **Which licences new sources may show and save** | Once users save BY-SA or rights-unclear images, the attribution duty travels with them | Accept CC0 and PD-mark first (SMK, Met, Smithsonian); add Commons BY/BY-SA only with the provenance sidecar; refuse NC-only and anti-harvest sources |
| **Q23: contacting Are.na** | First impressions with a platform are hard to redo | Yes, before any public expansion such as a web version, winget or the Store; it costs an email |
| **Q18/Q21 web hosting, Q22 mailer account** | Both publish under, or use, the owner's accounts | Park both; nothing in 1.1–1.3 depends on them |

## Conclusion

The audit changes the question. It is no longer "what feature is LeMuse missing?" but "which half of a reference app has LeMuse built?" It has built the practice half to a standard that matches or beats free web timers. It has barely started the reference-library half that boards like PureRef and Eagle trained artists to expect: look closer, use my files, hand the image to my painting app, keep its credit with it. That is why the app can feel complete and incomplete at once. The good news is that the library half is mostly small, permission-free or permission-light work, and none of it needs LeMuse to give up streaming, cache images or touch bot protection. Read with that lens, zoom, local folders and drag-out are not three features. They are one promise: "LeMuse plays well with the rest of your studio."

The second insight is about process, not product. The owner's sense that something is off partly comes from the app failing to tell its own story: the version doesn't move, nothing says what's new, and the plan still describes v0.7. Making the version, changelog and portable build checked by CI is the cheapest fix in this report, and it stops the feeling from coming back after every release. Three facts stay open and should be tested rather than assumed. Does EyeDropper work in LeMuse's WebView2? Do Krita, PureRef and Blender accept a dragged file from Tauri? Do the SMK and Met image hosts load cleanly in a normal browser? Each takes minutes to settle, and each decides whether a planned item ships as described.
